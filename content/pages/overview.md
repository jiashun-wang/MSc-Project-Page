---
title: Overview
---
Research in the Marine Heatwave & the Indonesian Seas program aims to understand the characteristics and long-term trends of marine heatwaves in the Indonesian Seas, and to identify the local dynamical drivers and large-scale climate forcings behind them. Our work is organized around three broad questions: What are the spatial and temporal characteristics of marine heatwaves in this region—their frequency, intensity, duration, and spatial extent? What are the long-term trends in these events under a warming climate? And what are the underlying mechanisms—both local ocean dynamics and remote climate modes—that drive their onset, maintenance, and decay? Using a combination of statistical analysis and heat budget analysis, we trace the pathways through which large-scale climate variability and regional ocean processes jointly give rise to extreme warming events in the Indonesian Seas. Across this work, a consistent theme emerges—that the Indonesian Seas, situated at the nexus of the Pacific and Indian Oceans, are not a passive recipient of basin-scale warming but a region where local dynamics and remote forcings interact in complex ways to shape the occurrence and evolution of marine heatwaves.
